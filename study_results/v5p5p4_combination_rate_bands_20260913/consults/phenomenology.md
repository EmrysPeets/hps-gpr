# Phenomenology consultation: the selected 92 MeV structure

12 September 2026. This memo assesses possible interpretations, not their evidence or exclusion. The inherited amplitude is a dark-photon-equivalent template normalization. No model-specific accepted-rate calculation, external-limit recast, or null calibration was performed.

## Candidate physical frameworks

**Kinetically mixed vector:** a narrow A' pole near 92 MeV can produce an electron pair by electron-nucleus bremsstrahlung. Its pole mass and couplings do not depend on the accelerator setting. Beam energy changes its boost, production phase space, angular acceptance and decay acceptance. The published production calculation does not imply a universal extra E^-2.904 factor after the existing radiative-trident normalization. For an illustrative minimal-vector benchmark with epsilon squared = 10^-6, Gamma(A' -> ee) approximately alpha epsilon squared m/3 = 0.224 eV at 92 MeV, far below a MeV detector width. This is a derived benchmark, not an inferred coupling or width. [Bjorken et al., Section II](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.0580).

**Electron-coupled scalar or pseudoscalar:** effective interactions g_S S ebar e and i g_P P ebar gamma5 e also permit bremsstrahlung production and two-body ee decay. The couplings, production matrix elements, energy-sharing distributions and accepted rates differ from a vector; photon couplings or additional decay channels can further change production and visible branching fractions. These are defensible alternative templates, but no cited calculation predicts the observed residual rate ratios. A generic electron scalar is not automatically the minimal Higgs-mixing model. Use the model's own generated events and branching/lifetime response before interpreting its fitted normalization. [Liu, McKeen and Miller, scalar production](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.06781), [Liu and Miller, spin-0/spin-1 production, Eq. (1) and Section III](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.01633).

**Interference:** the electron-nucleus ee final state requires coherent direct/exchange amplitudes; the relevant primary treatment gives the combined dark-photon/QED amplitude in Eq. (7). A physical test would integrate |M_QED + M_X|^2 over each campaign's selection and convolve with its detector response. An interference term may distort a line, but a free shifted Gaussian is not that calculation. As a general amplitude-level inference, a slowly varying real continuum gives an approximately odd dispersive term around a narrow pole, which largely cancels in symmetric integration; absorptive phases or asymmetric acceptance can change this. Consequently neither small fitted offsets nor rate disagreement establishes interference. [Beranek and Vanderhaeghen, Section II](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.5104).

**Detector or background response:** residual efficiency, material conversion, charge/track assignment, alignment, momentum scale, and background-shape errors can change both fitted rate and centroid. HPS explicitly uses Moller pairs and full-energy electrons as mass and momentum controls and distinguishes radiative tridents, Bethe-Heitler production and converted bremsstrahlung. These mechanisms motivate tests; they do not themselves predict a universal narrow 92 MeV structure. [HPS 2016 analysis, Sections III C--E](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10629).

## Mass offsets and MC-bounded widths

Write the reconstructed centroid as m_X + delta_i, keeping one physical pole. A displacement delta_i is a response nuisance, not evidence that particle mass changes with beam energy. Offset bounds need independently justified mass-calibration information; a fraction of the resolution chosen for this study is only a diagnostic range. If both m_X and every delta_i float without calibration information, they have a common-shift degeneracy; fix a reference or supply an identified calibration model.

The v5.3.1 source protocol gives the following **derived detector-sigma endpoints at 92 MeV**, in dataset order 2015/2016/2021: MC-side = **3.7770/1.9091/1.9589 MeV**, production-side = **4.8039/3.7884/2.4486 MeV**. These are not intrinsic particle widths or measured plus/minus-one-sigma uncertainties. The 2015 MC-side curve was inferred from the calibration chain; 2021 MC-side already includes hit/curvature smearing. The old exp(-t^2/2), 0<=t<=1 constraint explicitly assumed an MC-centered diagnostic penalty, with the production endpoint one unit away. Do not promote that assumption into a measured likelihood for calibration.

Use identical observed bins and background constraints across the width/offset profile. Integrate normalized signal templates into those fixed bins, retaining the changing fraction outside the window; renormalizing every truncated template to unity would entangle width with fitted rate. Report boundaries and constraint contributions. Preserve the exploratory 2015 extension above 90 MeV. These recommendations follow the local protocol and likelihood consistency, not a new literature calibration.

## Existing visible-resonance constraints

Representative primary searches cover 92 MeV: **A1/MAMI**, electron-pair production, 40--300 MeV; **BaBar**, e+e- -> gamma A', 20 MeV--10.2 GeV; and **NA48/2**, pi0 -> gamma A', 9--120 MeV. They reported no signal. A1 quotes sensitivity down to epsilon squared = 8e-7 somewhere in its range; that is not the exact 92 MeV limit. NA48/2 loses sensitivity toward its upper endpoint because pion-decay phase space contracts. [A1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.5502), [BaBar](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2980), [NA48/2, Section 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.00607).

These constraints make an external consistency check essential for a minimal visible dark photon. They are not direct exclusions on the study's proxy amplitude. A physical comparison needs production couplings, visible branching fraction, lifetime, resolution and accepted yield in each experiment. Pion limits depend on quark/meson couplings; scalar/pseudoscalar limits require their own recasts. Changing those assumptions does not automatically evade electron-production searches. This is a representative constraint map, not an exhaustive current exclusion compilation.

## What fitting the energy law does to significance

Let q(beta) be twice the improvement over background for a nonnegative common signal amplitude with R_i=(E_i/2.3 GeV)^beta. For an independently fixed law and a fixed template, the familiar one-amplitude asymptotic mapping requires its usual regularity and background-calibration assumptions. With beta fitted from these same spectra, the statistic is instead q_profile = max_beta q(beta); beta is absent at zero signal. Its null distribution is not automatically half-chi-square(1), nor can one fix it by assigning one extra Wilks degree of freedom. [Cowan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.1727), [Gross and Vitells, Section 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.1891).

Freezing the previously data-fitted beta=-2.9035846 in a follow-up using the same events does not remove the selection cost. Profiling mass offsets and unconstrained signal widths adds related adaptation. The raw likelihood improvement cannot decrease when enlarging a nested alternative, but its calibrated significance need not increase. Compare objective values and call sqrt(q) a nominal conditional root unless the complete selection/profile procedure is calibrated. A slope fixed using independent future/training data could support a separate test, with its uncertainty treated appropriately. The chosen 92 MeV mass also retains the earlier mass-search selection.

## Most useful discriminants

1. Test centroid and rate stability in matched opening-angle, pair-energy fraction, track-energy asymmetry, detector-half and hit-category bins; compare each with Moller/FEE calibration controls.
2. Generate vector/scalar/pseudoscalar hypotheses through each actual campaign configuration; predict accepted rate ratios before fitting these three amplitudes. Require a mechanism to reproduce residual response factors, not only a common mass.
3. Examine asymmetric residuals and a full coherent amplitude template before invoking interference. Vertex distributions test prompt versus finite-lifetime acceptance.
4. Validate any energy law or template choice on independent data. The three campaigns are confounded by target, detector, trigger and selection changes, so an empirical rate fit alone cannot identify a beam-energy mechanism.
