\section{Results for the released data}
\label{sec:selected-observed-results}
\input{../derived/generated_selected_results}

This is a snapshot of a partially unblinded analysis. It includes the fully unblinded
2015 and 2016 samples and the 10\% sample currently available from 2021. The remaining 2021 data are outside the reviewed sample. The curves below
describe the observed result in hand and do not project the full-2021 reach.

Each mass point uses the corrected, bounded, piecewise-asymptotic \CLs{}
construction with the common signal-strength coordinate constrained to
$\eps^2\geq 0$. The Gaussian-process supports, hyperparameter states, and physics
normalizations are frozen before the observed limits and $p_0$ values are evaluated.
The inference is conditional on those frozen choices and on
the asymptotic approximation. For 2016, the frozen GP support is
\FinalSixteenSupportLowMeV--\FinalSixteenSupportHighMeV~MeV and the upper
resolution-scaled length-scale factor is \FinalSixteenLengthScaleUpperFactor. The
2015 and 2021 configurations retain their separately qualified supports.
One numerical caveat applies to 2016. An independent cross-process replay passed the
specified $10^{-6}$ state-reproduction check at 87 of 142 masses. A downstream replay
with the optimizer disabled, together with the other release checks, passes; even so,
every 2016-inclusive curve remains preliminary and conditional on this exception.

The three current inputs can be analyzed in seven distinct combinations: each data set
alone, each of the three pairs, and all three together.

\subsection{Conditional full-2021 response compared with BaBar}
\label{sec:v501-babar-proxy}
To show how the current result responds to a larger 2021 sample, we repeat
the restricted density-response construction used in Figure~84 of v4.9.7.
Let $\rho_d(m)$ be the observed prompt normalization density in the physical
window $m\pm1.64\sigma_m$. Holding the full 2015 and 2016 samples fixed and
increasing only the 2021 contribution by a factor of ten gives
\begin{equation}
 (\epsilon^2_{90})_{\rm proxy}(m)
 = (\epsilon^2_{90})_{\rm current}(m)
 \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{d\in\mathcal D(m)}\rho_d(m)}
 {\sum_{d\in\mathcal D(m)}s_d\rho_d(m)}} ,
 \qquad (s_{2015},s_{2016},s_{2021})=(1,1,10).
 \label{eq:v501-density-proxy}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal D(m)$ is the active dataset set. The scale is unity below
50~MeV, density weighted where samples overlap, and exactly $1/\sqrt{10}$
where only 2021 contributes. The current and proxy minima are
$8.0043\times10^{-7}$ and $3.1898\times10^{-7}$, both at 72~MeV.
These are properties of the displayed curves, not expected future outcomes.

\fig{0.98}{../figures/v501_current_projected_babar.pdf}{Current observed
90\% upper limits and the conditional full-2021 density-response proxy
compared with the published BaBar visible-dark-photon observed 90\% contour
\cite{BaBarDarkPhoton2014}. The gray dashed curve is exactly the current
maximal-available-dataset HPS result over 19--250~MeV. The blue curve applies
Eq.~\eqref{eq:v501-density-proxy} to its current observed normalization
densities. It retains present fluctuations and is neither expected
sensitivity nor a future observed limit or a fit to full-2021 data.
HPS and BaBar use different statistical constructions. The inherited
dimuon correction and 2016 qualifications remain applicable.}
{fig:v501-babar-proxy}

\subsection{Signal-yield and coupling limits for each dataset}
\label{sec:v501-individual-scans}\label{sec:selected-individual-results}
Figures~\ref{fig:v501-scan-2015}--\ref{fig:v501-scan-2021} separate the
observed yield limit from its conversion to $\epsilon^2$. The upper panels
use the full Gaussian-template yield, while the likelihood uses the portion
inside the fitted window. The different mass dependence of the yield and
coupling limits follows from the dataset's mass-dependent normalization.
The nominal local minima occur at 51, 90, and 78~MeV in 2015, 2016, and
2021, with $p_0=8.46\times10^{-4}$, $3.09\times10^{-4}$, and
$2.47\times10^{-3}$, respectively. They are fixed-mass asymptotic quantities;
the coherent global calculation in Section~\ref{v5-sec:global-method} uses
a separately declared scan statistic and conditional background model.

\subsection{Combining data sets through a shared coupling}
\label{sec:selected-combined-results}

Figure~\ref{fig:combined-final-results} contains all three pairwise combinations and
the all-three result. At any given mass, the simultaneous likelihood profiles one
nonnegative $\eps^2$ shared by the active campaigns. Each campaign nevertheless
retains its own signal normalization, GP prediction, covariance, and observed
spectrum. Combining the data sets is thus a new likelihood calculation, not an
envelope, average, or choice among the standalone limits. Each curve is evaluated only
on the mass interval common to all of its active inputs.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.94\linewidth,height=0.59\textheight,keepaspectratio]{../figures/combined_final_results.pdf}
  \caption{Observed shared-$\eps^2$ results for the three pairwise combinations
  and the all-three overlap. For visual alignment, the top coordinate expresses the
  shared-coupling limit as the total compatible signal yield summed over the active
  campaigns. The middle panel gives the 90\% bounded, piecewise-asymptotic \CLs{}
  upper limit on $\eps^2$, and the bottom panel gives the local asymptotic $p_0$.
  No expected-limit bands are shown.}
  \label{fig:combined-final-results}
\end{figure}

The shared coupling expresses the physical relationship among the campaigns: a
putative dark photon has one $\eps^2$, while its expected event count
changes with beam conditions, exposure, acceptance, and mass resolution. It also
keeps the independent background information from each spectrum explicit.
