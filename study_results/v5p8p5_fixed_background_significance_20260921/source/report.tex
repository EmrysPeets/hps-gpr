\section*{What I would ask of more 2021 data}
My interest is in a reproducible mass, shape and rate. Removing background nuisance parameters changes the apparent ranking of the existing data, but supplies no new events. I would use the earlier profiled studies and the new fixed-background check together to design a replication, with the background policy chosen before inspecting that replication.

The earlier profiled studies made 90--93 MeV worth following for rate consistency, and 65--67 MeV worth following under a common coupling. At 92 MeV their individual fitted $\epsilon^2$, in units of $10^{-6}$, were
\[
77.48\pm29.96\;(2015),\qquad9.13\pm2.93\;(2016),\qquad1.65\pm1.35\;(2021).
\]
Those are conditional fit errors, not complete detector or normalization uncertainties. The profiled 2021 sample supplied about 82\% of the local null information there while preferring the smallest amplitude. That explains why a flexible combination can look stronger than the physically restricted one. A common location does not by itself establish a common physical rate.

\textbf{Use genuinely new events for the first replication test.} If the later total contains the examined 10\%, analyze the additional events separately before showing the pooled result. Freeze the mass hypotheses, signal widths, rate conversion, background treatment, test statistic and $\pm2.25\sigma_m$ mask. Searching intervals around nominated candidates still carries a trials correction, as does choosing among several candidates.

\textbf{At 90--93 MeV, compare the rate as well as the mass.} New 2021 events could support a resolution-shaped excess at the rate predicted by a qualified common-coupling model, or make the differences among campaigns clearer. At 65--67 MeV they test the earlier shared-coupling preference. The new fixed-background preference near 51 MeV must also be judged through its dataset composition and calibration, rather than treated as an independent confirmation of the earlier studies. Around 76--80 MeV, examine the neighboring residual pattern as a background-model control.

\textbf{More counts alone cannot distinguish signal from persistent model error.} A fractional background error has a residual proportional to exposure, just as a persistent signal fraction does. In a counting approximation,
\[
 Z_{\rm mismatch}(k)\simeq\frac{k\,\delta b}{\sqrt{k\,b}}
 =\sqrt{k}\,\frac{\delta b}{\sqrt b}.
\]
A growing nominal score can therefore reveal a modeling error more precisely. Check fractional residuals, peak width, sidebands and justified control selections, including possible signal absorption when constructing the generating source. The v5.0.5 full-exposure projections assume persistence of selected partial-sample excesses; they are scenarios, not forecasts of unseen data.
