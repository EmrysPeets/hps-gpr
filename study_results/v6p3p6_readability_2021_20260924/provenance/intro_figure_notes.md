# Introductory signal MC figures: source and caption facts

These figures regenerate saved v6.1 numbers without fitting or generating toys. `scripts/make_intro_figures.py` is self-contained after packaging: its copied inputs are in `provenance/intro_signal_mc/`, whose `source_sha256.json` records source hashes. All three PDF figures also have PNG previews in `figures/` and were visually inspected.

## intro_core_shift_comparison.pdf

Left: the 2021 v13 `ap_signal_prompt_smeared` selected target-constrained (TC) signal MC reconstructed invariant-mass core position, at generated masses 60–240 MeV in 20 MeV steps. Define c as the centre of a local bin-integrated Gaussian plus nonnegative linear pedestal fit to the saved signal MC (the pedestal represents the local tail contribution, not experimental background). The line is the saved v6.1 descriptive relation c-m = -3.2243308693 - 2.2139928114 ln[m/(150 MeV)], with c, m and their difference in MeV. Error bars are the standard deviation of the fitted centre over 32 signal-MC bin-resampling replicas, not standard errors of 100-toy means. These tiny errors can be smaller than markers. The curve was chosen by comparisons in which each mass sample was omitted from the relation fit and then predicted; these comparisons are model selection, not independent validation. The RMS omitted-mass prediction error was 0.0652 MeV. Right: author-supplied HPS Internal image reproduced unchanged, including its original series identifiers and error marks; its numerical inputs, error definition and selection equivalence were not supplied, so use it as a qualitative comparison. Do not claim the two panels are the same calibrated dataset.

## intro_signal_mc_catalogue.pdf

The eleven selected 2021 TC signal MC samples at generated masses 60–260 MeV are dark gray. The central mass Gaussian (red) is centred at the generated mass m; the shifted Gaussian (blue dashed), also called the log core Gaussian, is centred at the logarithmic c(m) above. Both Gaussian prescriptions use the analysis resolution sigma(m), not the narrower fitted signal-MC core width. In MeV, sigma(m)=1000[0.00184825 - 0.001375(m/1000) + 0.085875(m/1000)^2]. Signal MC bins divide by ALL selected events, including recorded overflow above 400 MeV; none of the eleven samples has underflow. The Gaussian densities use their full-distribution normalization. No curve is renormalized to a plotted interval or the histogram support. The explicit flow ledger is `intro_signal_mc/flow_normalization.json`. The signal MC shows non-Gaussian tails. The 260 MeV sample is shown for shape only and has no shifted curve because the fitted shift relation was validated over 60–240 MeV. No toy uncertainties or error bars appear. The excluded 40 MeV sample is not included. No independent generated 65 MeV sample exists in this v6.1 catalogue.

## intro_common_core_shape.pdf

For each 60–240 MeV signal MC sample, subtract its saved Gaussian-core fit centre c and divide by its saved fitted core width sigma_core; these are descriptive local signal-MC fits and sigma_core is distinct from the analysis resolution used in the extraction. Left: each standardized empirical curve is normalized to unit area within |u|<2, so the comparison asks about core shape alone. Right: probabilities divide by all selected events, including recorded overflow, retaining each sample's original core and tail probabilities. The left core conditioning cancels each overall denominator. The black dashed common shape is the equal-mass mean of the ten full-distribution empirical cumulative distributions, differentiated for display; the left common curve is then conditioned on |u|<2. The dotted gray reference is a standard Gaussian also conditioned on |u|<2. The figure uses the full-selected normalization of this study; the original v6.1 shape diagnostics quoted below used normalization within the saved histogram support. Overflow is less than 0.0153% and this normalization distinction does not change the conclusion. Curves are descriptive histograms, with no toy uncertainty bars.

The 80–240 MeV standardized cores are similar: when each mass is omitted in turn from the common-shape pool, the maximum cumulative-probability difference within |u|<2 ranges 0.00673–0.01371. At 60 MeV the corresponding difference is 0.04499. The 60 MeV sample has only 32.39% of selected probability within two fitted core widths, compared with 60.73–71.47% at 80–240 MeV. This supports the low-mass limitation discussed in connection with the geometry acceptance turn-on, but this plot alone does not isolate the physical cause. The user’s 65 MeV wording refers to this low-mass region; 65 MeV is an interpolated shape in the saved catalogue, not a separate generated sample. Similar cores do not establish a universal complete signal shape or equal acceptance.
