# MC-only exclusion study for v6.3.5

The likelihood fit region and the GP training exclusion are different objects. The fit region remains the bin-center mask within the MC logarithmic core location plus or minus 2.25 nominal resolution units. A wider exclusion removes extra bins from GP training without adding those bins to the likelihood. Every candidate exclusion contains the entire fit region.

At each native MC point, the equal-tail interval is the 2.5th through 97.5th percentiles of the full selected-event histogram distribution. The shortest interval is the shortest contiguous interval inside the recorded native histogram that contains 95% of all selected events. Histogram overflow stays in the total normalization. The 95% target is not a truncation of the signal distribution. All signal draws and full-yield definitions must retain both tails and all outside-support probability.

These continuous intervals are enlarged to contain every intersecting analysis bin and unioned with the fit region. The saved masks are the executable prescription. No observed-data excess, p-value, or upper limit enters their construction. The corresponding independent-toy study should compare frozen policies on exactly the same background and signal draws. It should quantify null offset, signal response, variance, coverage, and failure rate before choosing an exclusion policy.

The dimensionless coordinate is u = (m_rec - c_MC) / sigma_core. To express a physical mass interval with the proposed wiggle parameters, write [c_MC - 2 s L_wig, c_MC + 2 s R_wig], where s is an explicitly chosen resolution in MeV. The results separately tabulate parameters for s = nominal resolution and s = fitted core resolution. The variable u itself is not a width with mass units.

The logarithmic law is used only at native masses 60 through 240 MeV. The 260 MeV entry uses its directly fitted native MC core and is a shape diagnostic outside that law's validated domain; its archived kernel anchor would be 250 MeV. There is no 40 MeV entry.

The equal-tail 95% guards retain 95.0–95.3% of full selected MC but are very wide. At 60 MeV the exclusion spans 51.0–212.25 MeV; nominal-resolution widths across 60–240 MeV are about 17.5–77.7. All cases retain at least three external bins on each side. That geometric gate does not establish predictive adequacy across a large omitted interval.

The deterministic Asimov comparisons use the nominal GP truth and archived stress truth with the same frozen mass-specific kernel as the primary study. The injected expectation at each source and mass is three times the baseline null Asimov yield error. It is identical across guard policies. These are noise-free mean-spectrum diagnostics, not a held-out experiment or a coverage estimate. For the nominal source, equal-tail guards increase the returned null yield error by factors 1.39–6.08 relative to the core-fit guard; the shortest intervals increase it by as much as 29.3. The equal-tail paired Gaussian response ranges from 0.339 to 1.111. The selected-MC Gaussian projection and GP signal absorption are both present, so retention alone does not predict a yield correction.

The native MC metadata records v13 selected target-constrained mass and states that full v13/v16 selection equivalence is not established. The test is conditional on these supplied templates, rather than a validation of physical signal acceptance or data/MC selection agreement.

## Matched-window and paired-toy sensitivity check

The bounded extension compares four matched fit/exclusion windows about the same logarithmic core center, with left/right half-widths in nominal-resolution units (2.25,2.25), (2,2), (2.5,2), and (2,2.5). A fifth policy uses the original (2.25,2.25) fit window with the equal-tail 95% exclusion. These are fixed MC-only alternatives; no observed spectrum selected the candidates or their reported winner.

One hundred independent nominal-source pilot backgrounds determine the mean returned observed-Hessian error of the baseline Gaussian extraction at each mass. The next one hundred independent backgrounds receive full selected native MC with expectation A = 3 times that frozen pilot error. Background draws are shared across masses; signal draws are independent across masses and identical across the five policies. Both null and injected spectra are fitted under each policy, retaining signed yields. There are 1,000 pilot and 10,000 evaluation free fits; all pass numerical checks. Exact seeds, full signal draws, fit rows, and 200 atomic checkpoints are retained. Replay checks verify all checkpoint hashes, six background draws, three signal draws, and three complete fits.

Define R = mean(Ahat_injected - Ahat_null)/A and sigma_emp = SD(Ahat_null). The sensitivity proxy sigma_emp/R estimates full selected yield noise after local linear-response adjustment. It is compared to the same quantity for the baseline using a paired bootstrap over complete evaluation toy IDs (2,000 resamples). Returned-error/R is tabulated separately. This comparison is conditional on one background source, one injected strength, fixed templates, and fixed kernel policy; it is not an upper-limit coverage test.

The equal-tail 95% exclusion worsens sigma_emp/R by factors 1.15–3.54, with returned-error/R factors 1.23–4.52. Its extra retained MC does not preserve sensitivity. The matched-window alternatives produce empirical sensitivity ratios 0.983–1.014, with almost all 95% pointwise intervals spanning unity. The (2.5,2) window at 100 MeV gives 0.983 [0.972,0.997], one exploratory local comparison among thirty; no global or simultaneous evidence supports selecting that exception. There is no demonstrated robust sensitivity gain from changing the baseline.

Known-background Asimov controls give Gaussian projection response 0.378–0.857 even with exact background knowledge. The baseline GP response divided by this control is 0.703–0.974. Thus full selected MC-to-Gaussian shape projection and GP absorption are separate effects. Enlarging the training exclusion addresses only the latter, and does not guarantee unbiased Gaussian yield recovery.

The Asimov table also gives native CLs yield endpoints divided by the local Asimov response and their ratio to baseline. These are response-scaled diagnostic endpoints, not validated corrected upper limits. The empirical sensitivity proxies and this deterministic CLs diagnostic both reject adoption of the wide 95% guard as a sensitivity-preserving default. No candidate is adopted by this side study; the baseline prescription remains the comparison reference.
