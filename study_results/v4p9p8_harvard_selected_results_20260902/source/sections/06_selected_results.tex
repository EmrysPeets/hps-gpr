\section{Selected observed results}
\label{sec:selected-observed-results}
\input{sections/generated_selected_results}

This section brings the principal observed results into one compact comparison.
At each mass hypothesis, the upper curves answer two versions of the same question:
how many signal events remain compatible with the data under the 90\% \CLs{}
criterion, and what
upper limit does that imply for the kinetic-mixing parameter $\eps^2$?  The lower
curves show the corresponding local asymptotic background-only $p_0$.  A small
$p_0$ identifies an upward fluctuation worth examining; it is not, on its own, a
discovery probability for a scan over many masses.  Only observed curves are shown.
There are no expected-limit bands in any figure in this section.

The curves deliberately preserve two distinct analysis states.  The full-2015 and
full-2016 results, and every multi-campaign combination, use the reviewed historical
v4.2 state.  In that state, the 2021 contribution is the native 10\% sample with a
40--300~MeV Gaussian-process (GP) support.  The separate 2021 10\% individual curve
uses the later v4.9.5 result with the selected 36--300~MeV support.  It is displayed
as the most recent standalone 2021 result, but it is not substituted into the
historical combinations.  The 2016 10\% and 2021 1\% curves are likewise standalone
comparisons.  A later source-conditioned attempt to choose a new support for the
full-2016 statistics did not satisfy its acceptance rule, so it provides no updated
full-2016 observed result.  Retaining the reviewed v4.2 curve avoids turning that
failed optimization into an unqualified analysis choice.

\subsection{Individual data sets}
\label{sec:selected-individual-results}

Figure~\ref{fig:individual_results_triptych} compares five standalone scans: full
2015, 2016 10\%, full 2016, 2021 1\%, and 2021 10\%.  The upper panel reports the
signal-yield limit before translating it into a coupling; the middle panel applies
the campaign-specific luminosity, efficiency, resolution, radiative fraction, and
branching inputs needed for the $\eps^2$ limit.  Keeping both panels visible separates
what comes directly from the statistical fit from what also depends on the physics
normalization.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{../figures/individual_results_triptych.pdf}
  \caption{Standalone observed results for full 2015, 2016 10\%, full 2016,
  2021 1\%, and 2021 10\%.  From top to bottom, the panels show the 90\%
  asymptotic \CLs{} signal-yield upper limit, the corresponding electron-channel
  $\eps^2$ upper limit, and the local asymptotic $p_0$.  The full-2015 and
  full-2016 curves are the historical v4.2 results; the 2021 10\% curve is the
  later v4.9.5 standalone result.  No expected-limit bands are shown.  The marked
  50--52~MeV portion of the 2021 1\% scan is retained as a GP-support-edge
  diagnostic and is excluded from candidate interpretation.}
  \label{fig:individual_results_triptych}
\end{figure}

The lowest three mass hypotheses in the 2021 1\% scan, 50--52~MeV, lie immediately
above its lower search boundary and are sensitive to the treatment of the GP support
edge.  They are therefore shown for completeness but not promoted as candidate-level
evidence.  This distinction matters especially in a multi-curve plot: a formally
small local value at an analysis boundary can be visually striking even when it is
known to be a modeling diagnostic.

After those three edge points are set aside, the smallest local value in the
2021 1\% series occurs at \SelectedOnePercentInteriorMinMass~MeV,
$p_0=\SelectedOnePercentInteriorMinP$ ($Z=\SelectedOnePercentInteriorMinZ$).
In the current 2021 10\% series, the minimum occurs at
\SelectedCurrentTwentyOneMinMass~MeV,
$p_0=\SelectedCurrentTwentyOneMinP$ ($Z=\SelectedCurrentTwentyOneMinZ$).
Both are fixed-mass summaries rather than scan-wide significances.

\subsection{Shared-coupling combinations}
\label{sec:selected-combined-results}

The combinations in Figure~\ref{fig:combined_results_triptych} profile one common
$\eps^2$ while allowing each campaign to retain its own expected signal yield and
background model.  They are simultaneous likelihood results, not pointwise envelopes
or arithmetic averages of the standalone limits.  The three pairwise curves are full
2015 + 2021 10\%, full 2016 + 2021 10\%, and full 2015 + full 2016.  The all-three
curve is full 2015 + full 2016 + 2021 10\%, evaluated where all three campaigns
overlap.  It does not imply a 2021 full-statistics result.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{../figures/combined_results_triptych.pdf}
  \caption{Historical v4.2 shared-$\eps^2$ results for the three pairwise
  combinations and the common three-campaign overlap.  For visual alignment, the
  top coordinate multiplies the shared-$\eps^2$ limit by the sum of the active
  campaigns' signal normalizations.  The remaining panels show the observed
  90\% asymptotic \CLs{} upper limit on $\eps^2$, and the local asymptotic $p_0$.
  Each combination uses the reviewed v4.2 campaign states, including the 40--300~MeV
  GP support for the 2021 10\% contribution.  No expected-limit bands are shown.}
  \label{fig:combined_results_triptych}
\end{figure}

Using a common coupling is important physically.  A candidate signal would have the
same $\eps^2$ in each campaign, but its expected event count would change with beam
conditions, exposure, acceptance, and mass resolution.  The simultaneous likelihood
enforces that relationship while allowing each data set to contribute according to
its own reach.

\subsection{Local asymptotic scan}
\label{sec:selected-local-p0-series}

Figure~\ref{fig:asymptotic_pvalue_series} collects the local asymptotic $p_0$ series
without the two limit panels.  This view makes common mass-local structure easier to
compare across data sets and combinations.  Every point tests one fixed mass.  The
series has not been calibrated with scan-wide background-only pseudoexperiments, and
no look-elsewhere correction is assigned here.  Consequently, the plotted values are
local diagnostics rather than global significances.  They also should not be read as
coverage tests for the upper limits.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{../figures/asymptotic_pvalue_series.pdf}
  \caption{Local asymptotic background-only $p_0$ as a function of mass for the
  standalone scans and the historical v4.2 shared-coupling combinations.  The
  support-sensitive 50--52~MeV segment of the 2021 1\% scan is marked as a boundary
  diagnostic.  These are fixed-mass values: no scan-wide calibration or global
  significance is reported.}
  \label{fig:asymptotic_pvalue_series}
\end{figure}

\subsection{The largest all-three local fluctuation}
\label{sec:selected-all-three-extraction}

Within the historical v4.2 all-three overlap, the smallest local asymptotic value
occurs at \SelectedAllThreeMinMass~MeV:
$p_0=\SelectedAllThreeMinP$, corresponding to
$Z=\SelectedAllThreeMinZ$ under
the one-sided local asymptotic mapping.  Figure~\ref{fig:historical_all_three_extraction}
shows the underlying extraction separately in the three campaigns.  Presenting the
data, GP background, and fitted signal contribution together makes it possible to
see how a single shared coupling is supported---or constrained---by statistically
independent spectra.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{../figures/historical_all_three_m065_extraction.pdf}
  \caption{Historical v4.2 extraction at the 65~MeV mass hypothesis, the largest
  local upward fluctuation in the all-three overlap.  The three panels show the full
  2015, full 2016, and 2021 10\% spectra with their GP background descriptions and
  the signal contributions associated with standalone and shared-$\eps^2$ fits.
  The quoted $p_0$ and $Z$ are local asymptotic quantities.  This display is a
  candidate diagnostic, not a scan-calibrated discovery claim.}
  \label{fig:historical_all_three_extraction}
\end{figure}

Taken together, these plots show both the gain from combining campaigns and the
discipline required to interpret that gain.  Upper limits may be compared only within
their stated analysis configurations, while localized excesses require an additional
scan-wide calibration before they can support a global claim.
