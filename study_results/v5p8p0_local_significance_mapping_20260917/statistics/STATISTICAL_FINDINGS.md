## What the mapping does

For a fixed mass the raw signed likelihood root is r = sign(psi_hat) sqrt(2[ell(0)-ell(psi_hat)]). The nominal one-sided display uses Z=max(0,r), p=1-Phi(Z); this is an asymptotic reference, not independently calibrated by the covariance calculation. A stress reference supplies offset a=r(B) and response width s. Its standardized coordinate (r-a)/s is a tail relative to that reference. Large |a| signals nonclosure of that reference under the extraction. Centering it is not evidence that the reference is appropriate.

The positive-raw-fit gate additionally sets the conditional statistic to minus infinity for r<=0. This explains the abrupt combined feature near76 MeV. In particular, a detector-resolution line shape does not set the apparent width of a gated probability curve. Half-MeV evaluation may resolve that transition and correlation discretization better, but cannot alter a fixed-mass stress offset.

The archived v5.1.1 complete-spectrum experiment confirms the distinction between response propagation and mean closure. Median empirical/response SD ratios are .990, .999, .994 for 2015,2016,2021, while stress-offset RMS values are .655,5.648,.807. Their archived grids differ (2016 includes additional half steps), so these RMS values describe each stored field rather than a same-grid goodness-of-fit ranking. Covariance agreement can coexist with a strongly biased null mean. See archived_field_quality.csv.

## Common coupling is an information-weighted projection

For independent datasets d in the local quadratic approximation, let I_d be information about a common coupling, w_d=sqrt(I_d/sum I), and z_d its signed efficient score. Then z_common=sum w_d z_d. The same weighted sum of signed profile roots approximates the exact common-coupling root here to .00241 over all126 observed/stress combinations; efficient-score reconstruction agrees to .00056. I_d was computed after profiling the Gaussian background nuisance, S_d^T[diag(lambda_0)+L_d L_d^T]^-1 S_d, with the native-density coupling conversion retained.

At76 MeV, observed information fractions (2015,2016,2021) are (.00742,.28899,.70359). Their root contributions are (-.02877,-1.32237,+1.51668), giving .16553 versus exact .16557. Thus the 2016 deficit is diluted and canceled mainly by 2021. Under stress, the corresponding weighted contributions are (-.01488,-7.86924,-.81800), giving -8.70213 versus exact -8.70008. The smaller combined stress offset is ordinary projection, not improved individual background closure.

At92 MeV information fractions are (.00169,.17560,.82272), and the individual roots (2.58954,3.11542,1.22170) produce common r=2.52026. Independent unconstrained amplitudes give Q=17.90417; a common amplitude gives Q=6.35169, a likelihood loss of11.55248. This likelihood loss measures rate incompatibility under the fitted model. Releasing amplitudes changes the hypothesis and number of degrees of freedom. sqrt(Q_independent) is not a common-signal Gaussian significance. The exact nonnegative independent-amplitude statistic is also recorded and always exceeds the nonnegative common-amplitude statistic as nesting requires.

## Exposure mechanism control

For a fixed fractional shape discrepancy, signal-shaped bias scales roughly with exposure while its counting uncertainty scales approximately with sqrt(exposure); the bias in significance units can therefore grow approximately as sqrt(exposure). A deterministic exposure ladder of the **full2016 stress shape**, with frozen reviewed kernel coordinates, gives a(76)=(-4.521,-7.266,-10.352,-14.653) at exposure fractions(.1,.25,.5,1). This is near sqrt(exposure) scaling. At42 it changes from-3.524 to-9.215; at92 from-.719 to-2.443. Changes in GP conditioning prevent exact square-root scaling, particularly near117 where the offset changes sign. This is a mechanism control, not a historical2016 10% sample. Cross-year differences additionally involve shapes, supports, resolutions, and kernel policy.

## Length-scale test: a bias–variance tradeoff, not a universal repair

All seven anchors42,66,74,76,90,92,117 were evaluated at length-scale multipliers(.5,.75,1,1.25), keeping kernel amplitude fixed. The full nominal local-GP mean at each anchor and the coherent stress truth were each injected with0,2,5 times the original nominal Fisher yield uncertainty. The identical physical signal yields were used across policies. Every injected spectrum was passed through GP sideband conditioning again, including the signal tails in the sidebands. The local-GP control remains a mass-specific, data-derived plug-in truth; it is not one coherent global null.

Half the nominal length scale changes stress offsets42:-9.215→+1.533,66:+12.469→-1.914,74:-17.596→-7.898,76:-14.653→-7.949,90:-1.713→-4.613,92:-2.443→+1.286,117:+.606→+2.400. Thus greater flexibility reduces severe excursions at some anchors, but76 still fails badly and90/117 worsen. There is no globally preferred multiplier from this test.

The nominal observed signal uncertainty increases by factors2.02–2.99 at half length scale. Deterministic size-five injected-yield recovery stays95.3–99.9%, and across the complete ladder/truth grid stays95.3–99.9%; consequently most sensitivity loss here is increased uncertainty, not wholesale signal absorption. Half-scale observed roots90 and117 drop from3.425 and3.279 to.626 and.561. These changes are reported as model dependence, not selected for favorable observed significance. Two- and five-strength numerical values are in stiffness_scan.csv.

At every anchor the reviewed nominal kernel has the greatest observed-sideband log marginal likelihood among this finite ladder. Half-scale alternatives lose43–52 log-likelihood units and .75-scale alternatives lose9.5–14.0. This is the exact sideband GP marginal likelihood using the existing zero mean and count-derived errors; it is a training-set model score, not a guarantee of masked-window signal closure. Kernel amplitude was not reoptimized, so these are targeted stiffness perturbations rather than a complete alternative-kernel fit.

This distinction complements the paper expert's audit: the historical upper-length-scale range test found zero boundary occupancy at kmax12/15/20 and negligible12→15 changes. Passing that test says the upper optimization bound was nonbinding. It does not rule out excessive interpolation stiffness for a particular alternative background shape, nor certify fixed-mass likelihood-root centering. Shortening length scale changes the substantive background model even when the optimizer had already converged.

## Statistical release recommendation

Retain the nominal local asymptotic roots as descriptive reference. For a new conditional local probability, directly compare raw r_obs with independent repeated complete-spectrum fits from each explicitly named background truth; keep all mass-specific references separate and report both k/N and add-one (k+1)/(N+1) with exact binomial intervals. When r_obs<=0, an inclusive q0=0 tail is1; retain nominal p=.5 only as its explicitly different display convention. The maximum p over two named source-conditioned truths can be a finite-family conservative diagnostic, but does not cover background estimation uncertainty or an untested physical family. AtN=1024, zero exceedances only imply p<.00292 at one-sided95% confidence, never a resolved tiny tail.

The numerical and theoretical work supports a useful new conditional stress/local-control comparison, and identifies the 2016 mean model as the limiting issue. It does not qualify a revised particle-local significance, an optimized shorter-length-scale release, or a new global significance.
