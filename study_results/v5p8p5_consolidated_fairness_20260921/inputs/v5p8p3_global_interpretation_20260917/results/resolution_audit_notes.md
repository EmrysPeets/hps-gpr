# Resolution and trial-factor audit

The saved global calculation already accounts for very strong mass-to-mass correlation. Median correlations between adjacent 0.5 MeV hypotheses are 0.9705 (2015), 0.9897 (2016), 0.9880 (2021), and 0.9856 (combined). These are correlated tests of the same data, not independent datasets. No independent-bin correction was used: the saved field covariance is propagated from the complete count response through GP retraining and the profiled likelihood.

At the observed peaks, tail-equivalent independent-test counts are 60.6, 56.7, 76.8, and 111.6, versus 163, 283, 401, and 463 grid points. The equivalent count is defined by matching the maximum-tail probability, N_tail=log(1-p_global)/log(1-p_local), and depends on the threshold. It is not an integer count of resolution elements. The much smaller covariance participation ranks, 7.82, 9.16, 15.74, and 20.14, characterize variance distribution among modes and cannot replace an extreme-value correction.

There are two separate limitations in the intuitive width/FWHM estimate. First, maxima can occur anywhere along a continuous correlated scan, including between arbitrarily chosen resolution-sized bins. Even a smooth, known-background Gaussian-template field has correlation exp[-Delta m^2/(4 sigma^2)] and, at high thresholds, a maximum-tail approximation sf(u)+C exp[-u^2/2]. Here C is the resolution-metric integral given in resolution_audit.json. Its effective tail count rises with u. At the observed 2015/2016/2021 peaks, independent FWHM-spaced tests would give global probabilities 0.00542/0.02953/0.12479; the smooth-template upcrossing approximation gives 0.01347/0.06521/0.25926. The actual fitted-response field gives 0.02345/0.10488/0.33352. Thus continuous smooth maxima explain a substantial part of the difference from FWHM counting before any moving-mask effects are invoked.

Second, the real fitted statistic is more structured than the overlap of positive signal templates. Its count-response covariance has pronounced negative lobes, reaching about -0.85, and decorrelates more quickly than the simple Gaussian-template benchmark. The statistic compares a peak region with a background estimated from surrounding data, so shifting the hypothesis changes both the signal template and which fluctuations influence the fitted background. The saved response includes moving native-bin masks, count-dependent GP prediction and covariance, reviewed mass-dependent kernel states, and changing dataset membership in the joint scan. These can affect the correlation geometry; this saved-field audit does not isolate their individual causal contributions. In particular, it cannot demonstrate that native-bin mask switching is the main cause.

A finite-grid geometric diagnostic, sum arccos(rho_adjacent)/(2 pi), is 2.05, 1.91, and 1.68 times the ideal Gaussian-template path metric for 2015, 2016, and 2021. This is a roughness comparison, not a calibrated trials count or a continuum convergence claim. Every adjacent 2015 and 2016 mass step changes native-bin mask membership; 389/400 steps do so for 2021. A separate fixed-mask ablation would be necessary to isolate that effect.

All numerical work here reuses the saved v5.8.2 fields and their 200,000 Gaussian maxima. No additional fits or random simulations were performed. The FWHM comparison for the combined scan uses the finest available detector resolution at each mass, solely as an illustrative heuristic; there is no claim that this is the resolution of the common-coupling fit. All actual probabilities retain the fixed-source conditional qualification of v5.8.2.
