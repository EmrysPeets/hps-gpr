\subsection{Empirical limit-tail diagnostics and unresolved tails}
\label{sec:v5-tail-results}

A second set of probabilities locates an observed \emph{upper limit} within its
conditional background-only distribution. A low-limit tail describes an
unusually strong observed constraint; a high-limit tail describes an unusually
weak constraint. Neither is the local probability of a narrow positive signal.
The two-sided diagnostic doubles the smaller tail, capped at one.

The targeted refinement added independent batches of 3,000 or 10,000 draws at
108 sparse scope--mass coordinates, for 660,000 additional scope evaluations.
It retained the original 300-toy bands. Nine zero-count cells remained after
the refinement. Zero exceedances therefore appear as one-sided 95\% Monte Carlo
upper bounds, not as zero probabilities. The requested further continuation
is outstanding: this editorial session generated no new toys and does not
replace unresolved tails with extrapolated probabilities.

\fig{0.98}{../figures/v5_individual_tail_probabilities.pdf}{Empirical limit-tail
diagnostics for each dataset. Unrefined points use 300 toys; selected coordinates
use the saved independent 3,000- or 10,000-toy batches. Open downward triangles show
one-sided 95\% Monte Carlo upper bounds when no tail event is observed; for the
two-sided diagnostic the bound is doubled and capped at one. These probabilities
refer to the upper-limit statistic and are distinct from local asymptotic $p_0$.}
{fig:v5-individual-tails}
\fig{0.98}{../figures/v5_combination_tail_probabilities.pdf}{The same empirical
limit-tail diagnostics for the pairwise and all-three combinations. Finite
estimates and zero-count bounds remain visually distinct. Additional sampling
can improve the precision of these conditional diagnostics, but cannot
establish physical background validity or a discovery probability.}
{fig:v5-combination-tails}

\subsection{Varying the mass resolution in the signal template}
\label{sec:v5-resolution-results}

The nominal resolutions were introduced with the detector inputs in
Section~\ref{sec:event-selection}. The saved width study repeats the 2021
extraction for templates with widths 0.8, 0.9, 1.0, 1.1 and 1.2 times nominal
at all 201 tested masses. It holds the GP mean and covariance, training
exclusion, fit bins and full-template yield-to-coupling conversion fixed.
Thus the background is still profiled in the local likelihood, but the GP
training is not repeated for a changed exclusion width.

All 1,005 saved mass--width limits pass their recorded numerical checks. The
nominal calculation reproduces the production limits within a relative
$1.28\times10^{-6}$. Across the grid, the median limit ratios at 0.8 and 1.2
times nominal are 0.799 and 1.202; at 78~MeV they are 0.687 and 1.329.
The final result retains the nominal template. These variations describe
sensitivity to the assumed shape; their envelope is not a systematic-error
band or a likelihood profile of detector-resolution uncertainty.

\fig{0.98}{../figures/v5_resolution_width_limits.pdf}{2021 observed 90\% \CLs{}
limits for the five signal-template widths, with ratios to the nominal limit
below. Every curve uses the same archived GP prediction and yield conversion.
The visible-branching correction is common to all curves and cancels in the
ratios. The plots retain all 1,005 saved limits.}
{fig:v5-resolution-limits}
\fig{0.98}{../figures/v5_resolution_width_significance.pdf}{Local significance
under the same template-width variations. The plotted signed local coordinate is
$r=\operatorname{sign}(\widehat A)\sqrt{2[\mathrm{NLL}(0)-\mathrm{NLL}(\widehat A)]}$;
its negative branch describes deficits. The 1.2-width template changes the
78~MeV value from 2.810 to 3.216 and the 71~MeV value from $-4.019$ to $-4.239$.
The 66~MeV reference changes from 2.366 to 2.478 for the 0.8-width template.}
{fig:v5-resolution-response}
Selecting a preferred mass or width after examining these curves does not add
independent evidence for a resonance or measure a resolution miscalibration.
