# Figure captions

## `v4p2_babar_observed_equivalent_projection_eps2`

Observed 90% upper limits on the minimal-visible dark-photon kinetic-mixing
parameter epsilon squared. The gray dashed curve is the reviewed HPS-GPR v4.2
observed asymptotic CLs result from the full 2015 and 2016 samples and the 2021
10% sample. The blue curve is an observed-equivalent response projection that
holds 2015 and 2016 fixed and scales only the 2021 normalization-window
density from 10% to 100% statistics. The orange curve is the published BaBar
visible-dark-photon observed 90% limit from Lees et al., *Phys. Rev. Lett.*
**113**, 201801 (2014). The HPS projection preserves fluctuations in the
current observation; it is not an expected sensitivity, a future observed
limit, or a refit to full-2021 data. HPS and BaBar use different statistical
constructions. No HPS limit bands or projected p-values are shown.

## `v4p2_babar_observed_equivalent_projection_eps2_with_projected_over_babar_ratio`

The upper panel repeats the reviewed HPS-GPR v4.2 and published BaBar 2014
visible-dark-photon observed 90% comparison. The lower panel shows the
pointwise projected HPS observed-equivalent proxy divided by the BaBar limit,
using linear interpolation in log epsilon squared for the BaBar contour and
no extrapolation. The horizontal line at one denotes equal numerical limits;
values below one identify masses where the projected HPS proxy is numerically
stronger than BaBar. The pale lower region emphasizes that comparison without
placing explanatory text over the ratio curve. The
projection holds 2015 and 2016 fixed and scales only the observed 2021
normalization-window density from 10% to 100% statistics. It preserves the
current observed fluctuations and is not an expected sensitivity, a future
observed limit, or a refit to full-2021 data.

## `v4p2_babar_observed_equivalent_projection_ratio`

Pointwise HPS limits divided by the BaBar 2014 visible-dark-photon contour,
using linear interpolation in log epsilon squared for the ratio only and no
extrapolation. The horizontal line marks equal numerical limits; the pale
region below unity denotes masses where the HPS curve is numerically lower.
The current v4.2 curve remains above BaBar on the HPS grid. The
observed-equivalent 2021 100%-statistics proxy crosses below BaBar in several
disconnected, fluctuation-derived intervals; these intervals are not
probabilities of future reach.
