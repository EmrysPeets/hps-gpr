\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section summarizes the corrected limit and projection products used for the
current internal review. The plots shown here are copied from the corrected CSV-driven
summaries so that the note, Overleaf upload, and local compilation use the same figure
inputs. The section is organized around five reader questions:
\begin{itemize}
\item What are the individual dataset inputs?
\item What is the corrected simultaneous shared-$\eps^2$ limit?
\item Which observed fluctuations affect the limit and discovery diagnostics?
\item What full-statistics reach is projected?
\item How does that projection compare with published HPS and BaBar exclusions?
\end{itemize}

The central result remains a simultaneous shared-$\eps^2$ analysis. At a fixed mass
hypothesis a single coupling value predicts a dataset-dependent signal yield through
the conversion factors of Sections~\ref{sec:methodology} and
\ref{sec:event-selection}. The individual 2015, 2016 10\%, and 2021 1\% limits are
therefore useful diagnostics and comparison curves, but the combined curve is not made
by taking the envelope of those individual upper limits. It is the direct result of the
profiled simultaneous likelihood in the common coupling parameter.

The ratio in Figure~\ref{fig:results-combined-best-ratio} makes the same diagnostic
more quantitative. It plots the observed combined upper limit in $\eps$ divided by the
best, meaning lowest, active individual observed upper limit in $\eps$ at each mass.
Values below one indicate that the simultaneous observed result improves on every
active single-dataset observed limit. Values above one indicate that, at that mass, the
best individual dataset alone has a stronger observed exclusion than the simultaneous
fit. This can happen for observed limits because the combined test must accommodate the
data in all active spectra at one common coupling; it is not evidence by itself that
the expected simultaneous sensitivity is worse. It is instead a useful audit of where
observed upward fluctuations, active-dataset changes, or conversion-factor differences
drive the shape of the combined observed curve.

\subsection{Full-statistics projections and published HPS comparison}
\label{sec:results-full-projections}

The projected full-data reach is an observed-equivalent scaling study. The 2015 sample
is held fixed, the 2016 10\% subset is scaled to 100\%, and the 2021 1\% subset is
scaled to 100\%. The scaling is applied through the local density/exposure factor used
in the yield-to-$\eps^2$ conversion, so the projected $\eps^2$ limit scales as
$\sqrt{\rho_{\mathrm{current}}/\rho_{\mathrm{projected}}}$ at each mass before the
comparison curves are drawn. This is a sensitivity projection, not a substitute for a
new observed full-statistics simultaneous fit.

Figure~\ref{fig:results-projection-published-combined} compares the projected
2015$+$2016$+$2021 full-data simultaneous reach with the published 2016 HPS upper
limit. The lower ratio panel shows that the full-data simultaneous projection is
substantially stronger than the published 2016 result over most of the plotted range;
the median projected/published ratio is about 0.11, corresponding to a median
improvement factor of about 9.2 in $\eps^2$.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\graphicorplaceholder{0.92\linewidth}{final_limit_projection_figs/projections/published_2016_vs_projected_combined_100pct_eps2.png}
\caption{Projected 2015$+$2016$+$2021 100\% simultaneous $\eps^2$ reach compared with
the published 2016 HPS upper limit. The ratio panel shows the projected limit divided
by the published limit, so values below one indicate an improvement relative to the
published 2016 result.}
\label{fig:results-projection-published-combined}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:results-projection-published-2016} isolates the 2016 scaling by
promoting the current 2016 10\% observed-equivalent curve to 2016 100\%. This is a
cleaner check of exposure scaling because it does not mix detector configurations or
mass ranges. The median projected/published ratio is about 0.71, so the 2016-only
100\% projection improves on the published curve on average but not uniformly at every
mass.

\subsection{BaBar comparison}
\label{sec:results-babar-comparison}

The observed 90\% upper-limit contour used in
Figure~\ref{fig:results-babar-projection} is the contour provided by the BaBar
collaboration for the visible-dark-photon search. For a like-for-like visual comparison
to the HPS 95\% CL curves, the plotted BaBar curve is converted from a 90\% limit on
$\eps$ to an approximate 95\% limit on $\eps^2$ with the one-sided Gaussian factor
\begin{equation}
\eps^2_{95}=\eps^2_{90}\frac{1.64485362695}{1.28155156554}
=1.28348610\,\eps^2_{90}.
\end{equation}
This is an approximate confidence-level conversion, but it is the appropriate
normalization for a faithful 95\%-versus-95\% visual comparison in the absence of the
original BaBar likelihood.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\graphicorplaceholder{0.92\linewidth}{final_limit_projection_figs/babar_95_comparison/babar95_vs_projected_full_data_100pct.png}
\caption{Projected full-data HPS simultaneous reach compared with the BaBar observed
visible-dark-photon exclusion converted to 95\% CL on $\eps^2$
\cite{BaBarDarkPhoton2014}. The lower panel shows projected HPS divided by BaBar.
Values below one indicate projected HPS reach beyond the BaBar curve. In this
projection HPS improves on BaBar in several low-mass pockets, but BaBar remains
stronger over much of the high-mass range.}
\label{fig:results-babar-projection}
\end{figure}

\subsection{2021 smeared versus unsmeared observed-limit comparison}
\label{sec:results-2021-smearing-comparison}

The 2021 target-constrained smearing scale discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:massres-2021} has also been propagated through the 90\% CLs
production chain as a dedicated resolution-systematics comparison. In this study the
2015 and 2016 inputs are unchanged, while the 2021 mass-resolution model is scaled by
the target-constrained data/MC factor. The comparison is therefore a controlled check
of how the combined upper limit, local discovery p-values, toy-limit tail p-values,
and Gaussian-equivalent significances respond when the 2021 signal template is
broadened.

Figure~\ref{fig:results-scaled-21-limit-comparison} compares the dimuon-corrected
scaled-resolution result with the unscaled reference distribution. The upper
panel overlays the observed and expected-median limits, while the lower panel shows
the scaled/unscaled ratio. Because the same dimuon branching factor is applied to both
curves at a common mass, the ratio isolates the effect of the 2021 resolution change
and the associated refit, not the branching correction itself. The observed
scaled/unscaled ratio has a median value of about 1.08 over the common scan points,
with the most visible structure driven by observed fluctuations rather than a smooth
normalization shift. The expected-median ratio is smoother and remains close to
unity, with the largest band-fixed excursions moving away from the repaired 60, 168,
and 187 MeV points.

\graphicorplaceholder{0.92\linewidth}{final_limit_projection_figs/90cls/scaled_21/scaled_21_bandfixed_dimuon_vs_unscaled_overlay_ratio.png}

\label{fig:results-scaled-21-limit-comparison}

The discovery-oriented comparison in
Figure~\ref{fig:results-scaled-21-significance} shows that the effect of the
resolution smearing is not monotonic. Several regions of the distribution become more
signal-like after the 2021 resolution is broadened: for example, the local $p_0$ is
smaller in the scaled study near 79, 96, 118, and 138 MeV. At the same time, many of
the more visually prominent upward fluctuations become less significant. The
unscaled feature near 141 MeV moves from a local $p_0\simeq2.1\times10^{-3}$
($Z\simeq2.87$) to $p_0\simeq6.2\times10^{-3}$ ($Z\simeq2.50$) after scaling, and
the excess-like structure around 169--170 MeV is similarly reduced. The minimum
scaled local $p_0$ in this comparison is about $4.9\times10^{-3}$ at 140 MeV
($Z\simeq2.58$). Thus the scaled-resolution treatment redistributes local
signal-like behavior across the scan rather than uniformly increasing or decreasing
the apparent significance.
