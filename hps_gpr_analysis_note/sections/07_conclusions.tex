\section{Conclusions and Outlook}
\label{sec:conclusions}

Version 3 records the current HPS GPR prompt-resonance analysis in a form aligned with
the July 2026 pre-unblinding studies. The 2021 analysis now uses the regenerated
\path{final_1pct_invM.root} input, rejects events satisfying
$p_{\mathrm{sum}}<\SI{2.8}{GeV}$, requires at least eight
two-dimensional hits on the electron track, and applies no vertex-fit $\chi^2$
selection. The result therefore no longer inherits the oppositely selected
low-$p_{\mathrm{sum}}$ development histogram or the earlier vertex and hit
requirements.

The GP length-scale bounds are also dataset dependent. The frozen lower factors are
1.0 for 2015, 0.9 for 2016, and 1.1 for 2021, with upper factors 8, 8, and 9,
respectively. Their interpretation is deliberately narrower than \emph{best value in
every metric}. The 2016 choice reduces the coherent pull bias seen for the earlier
bound; the 2015 choice is a conservative frozen baseline despite a marginally better
aggregate score at 1.1; and the 2021 choice is a calibration--reach compromise among
nearly degenerate candidates. The corresponding error-barred, matched-reference
functional-form studies are the principal closure figures in the note. Pull widths
below unity are described as conservative only conditional on a toy ensemble that
faithfully reproduces the production refit and fluctuation model.

The staged review candidate is a simultaneous 2015 full, 2016 10\%, and regenerated
2021 1\% 90\% CL upper limit on $\eps^2$ obtained with \CLs{}. The conversion uses
fixed radiative-fraction penalties of 7\%, 7\%, and 4.6\%, respectively, and includes
the minimal-model dimuon-threshold branching correction. The regenerated 2021
signal-yield and $\eps^2$ panels are shown together so that extraction and conversion
effects remain distinguishable. The combined result is obtained from the profiled
shared-coupling likelihood; it is not an envelope of individual observed limits.

The current band and observed curves remain pre-unblinding candidates. Their plotting
bundle records a reproducible display-level repair for isolated failed mass points:
in the displayed observed-limit curves only, log-linear interpolation at 24~MeV in
2015; 44, 98, 133, and 171~MeV in the 2016
10\% sample; and 136~MeV in the simultaneous observed limit. The repaired 136~MeV
value is used consistently in the headline limit, the individual-versus-combined
comparison, and the complete-campaign projection. Known failed fits are omitted,
rather than interpolated, in the individual local-$p_0$ scans. These choices
produce legible review figures without treating a failed export as a physical
measurement, and the repaired points and neighboring inputs are retained in the
figure audit tables. The affected hypotheses must nevertheless be regenerated or
reconciled from the frozen likelihood outputs before a final observed exclusion is
quoted. The connected local-$p_0$ curves and their analytic
effective-trials conversion provide the review-level global $p$-values used in v3.
A background-only maximum-$q_0$ ensemble would remain a valuable publication-stage
cross-check of that conversion, but it is not required to interpret the present
scan-level comparison.

The full-dataset comparison is an observed-equivalent density projection from the
same candidate. It holds the fully analyzed 2015 sample fixed and scales the 2016 10\%
and 2021 1\% samples to their corresponding complete campaign datasets by factors of
10 and 100. It is not a fit to the complete 2016 or 2021 datasets and is not an
expected production sensitivity. The
remaining publication gates are therefore concrete: regenerate the discrepant scan
points and expected bands from frozen outputs; validate direct \CLs{} coverage with
production-faithful toys; freeze and document the effective-trials conversion used for
the global $p$-values; and rerender every result figure from the same immutable
configuration and tables used for the final unblinding. The older 95\% results,
earlier closure suites, repair
diagnostics, and superseded projections remain available in
Appendix~\ref{app:prior-validation-results} as analysis history rather than headline
evidence.
