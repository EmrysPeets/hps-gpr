\section{Conclusions and Outlook}
\label{sec:conclusions}

Version 4 makes the endpoint treatment explicit. The signal search remains
\SIrange{19}{90}{MeV} in 2015, \SIrange{39}{180}{MeV} in 2016, and
\SIrange{50}{250}{MeV} in 2021, while the corresponding GPs are trained on
\SIrange{14}{135}{MeV}, \SIrange{30}{210}{MeV}, and
\SIrange{40}{300}{MeV}. These supports were declared before examining the v4
observed scan. They use populated sidebands beyond both search endpoints without
turning those sidebands into additional signal hypotheses.

The v4 combination uses the full 2015 and 2016 samples and the reviewed 2021 10\%
sample. At each mass, one shared $\eps^2$ predicts a campaign-specific signal yield,
so the observed 90\% confidence-level asymptotic \CLs{} curve is a simultaneous
likelihood result rather than an envelope or a luminosity rescaling of earlier
limits. Suspicious optimizer branches are handled by repeated fits with the card held
unchanged and by a recorded branch review. No observed point, expected quantile, or
$p$-value is supplied by interpolation.

The wider-support audit also exposes a feature that should remain visible in review:
the 2016 length scale sits on its frozen upper bound at all 142 search hypotheses.
That behavior reproduces across unchanged-card attempts, so it is not treated as a
missing optimizer branch. It is instead a clear reason to require support-matched
functional-form closure and direct coverage before promoting the conditional result
to a fully calibrated statement. The observed wide-to-narrow comparison is retained
as a support-dependence diagnostic, not used to choose whichever domain happens to
give the tighter curve.

The expected median and central 68\% and 95\% bands use 300 background-only joint
pseudoexperiments at each mass. The observed-data GP state is fixed throughout this
ensemble: pseudo-spectra fluctuate the conditional background prediction and event
counts, but the GP is not retrained on each toy. The bands are therefore a conditional
description of expected upper limits, not a measurement of full-procedure coverage.
Their empirical tail resolution is one toy, $1/300\simeq0.0033$, and a zero-count
tail is retained as 0 of 300 rather than promoted to a probability of zero.

The same ensemble supplies $p_{\mathrm{strong}}$, $p_{\mathrm{weak}}$, and the
bounded $p_{\mathrm{two}}$. These values answer whether the observed upper limit is
unusually strong or weak relative to the conditional background-only limit
distribution. They do not answer whether the data contain a resonance. That question
is summarized separately by the fixed-mass asymptotic $p_0$ scan. Its
\v{S}id\'ak-equivalent curve is an analytic effective-trials approximation and is not
a global significance calibrated by the 300 expected-limit toys.

The v3 result remains useful as analysis history: it established the statistical
definitions and shared-coupling construction, and it records why display-level
repairs were once used while reruns were pending. Its numerical bands, p-values,
sample fractions, and repaired points do not enter v4. The present result is rebuilt
from the wider-support scans and the reviewed full-2015, full-2016, and 2021-10\%
inputs.

Two validation steps remain distinct from the result reported here. Production-faithful
toys that retrain the GP are needed to test full-procedure \CLs{} coverage under the
frozen v4 card. A scan-wise background-only maximum-$q_0$ ensemble would be needed to
replace the analytic \v{S}id\'ak approximation with a toy-calibrated global discovery
$p$-value. Neither is inferred from the conditional 300-toy expected-band ensemble.
The eventual full-2021 unblinding is likewise a new analysis input, not a scale factor
to be applied to the present 10\% result.
