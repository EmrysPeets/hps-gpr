\title{Gaussian-process resonance search for prompt dark photons with the Heavy Photon Search experiment}

\author{HPS Collaboration}
\affiliation{Thomas Jefferson National Accelerator Facility, Newport News, Virginia 23606, USA}
\affiliation{SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory, Stanford, California 94309, USA}

\date{June 26, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We present a Gaussian process regression search for a narrow prompt
$\Ap \to e^+e^-$ resonance in invariant-mass spectra collected by the Heavy Photon
Search (HPS) experiment at Jefferson Lab. HPS is a fixed-target forward
spectrometer designed to search for visible dark-sector decays using a silicon vertex
tracker and an electromagnetic calorimeter. The analysis models the smooth quantum
electrodynamics trident background with a sideband-trained Gaussian process and tests,
at each mass hypothesis, for a detector-resolution-limited Gaussian signal. The
Gaussian process prediction supplies both the expected background and its correlated
uncertainty in the excluded signal window, which are propagated through a profiled
likelihood. Upper limits are set with the modified-frequentist $\CLs$ construction and
converted to limits on the kinetic-mixing coupling $\eps^2$ using the
radiative-trident normalization. Independent HPS samples are combined in a common
$\eps^2$ parameter rather than by combining precomputed limits. The present draft
documents the staged 2015, 2016, and 2021 GPR workflow, its validation program, and
the publication-scale figure set; final discovery claims require the frozen
full-statistics samples and scan-level look-elsewhere calibration.
\end{abstract}
